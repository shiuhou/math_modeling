2    Question 2: Capacity-valid scheduling

2.1 Contract and Algorithm 1

Each GPU model is simulated as its own pool. A job starts at sj ≥ aj , completes at fj = sj + pj , and
all Wj workers start together. At a proposed start time, a placement xji is admitted only if the pre-start
residual capacities satisfy

The resulting full-time model is instead the active-job constraint j:sj ≤t<fj gj xji ≤ Gcap
                                                                                        i   and its CPU
analogue. Thus a single job’s check against residual capacity is not written as a whole-cluster dynamic
constraint. Durations are exact integer seconds; gang admission is atomic, non-preemptive and non-
migrating.
Algorithm 1 (SPT-HOL): For each model pool, insert only arrived jobs into a shortest-processing-time
heap keyed by (pj , aj , idj ). Examine the heap head. If it is infeasible on the current residual nodes, stop
dispatching this pool at this timestamp (head-of-line blocking). If feasible, scan nodes in the selected
placement order, verify every worker slot’s GPU and CPU residual, then admit all workers atomically
and schedule one completion event. For GPU-series-1 only, the formal C0 case uses a CPU/GPU-ratio
node order before the same full slot check; every other pool uses ascending-ID ﬁrst ﬁt. Advance to the
next arrival or completion and repeat.
The historical program label SJF-HOL means this same method; the paper name is uniformly SPT-HOL.
SPT-FILL uses the same key but may inspect later feasible jobs under the ﬁxed K = 200 bypass budget.
A heap insertion or removal costs O(log n); a dispatch also scans candidate nodes and worker slots, and
ratio placement orders the candidate nodes by shape. Multiple starts at one timestamp can repeat these
scans, so an O(log n) event claim would be incomplete. Saved schedules were independently checked by
route, physical-capacity and metric validators.
The simultaneous-release mechanism has a precise necessary condition. If f − is the number of free
worker slots immediately before an event, ∆f is the number released by jobs ﬁnishing at that event, and
k is the next job width, admission requires

                                               f − + ∆f ≥ k.                                               (3)

Only with a continuously feasible single-worker backlog and zero free slots after the preceding dispatch
does this reduce to ∆f ≥ k. A ﬁnite K candidate budget can leave f − > 0, so the reduction is not
generally valid. This is a mechanism explanation for some zero-wait observations, not the unique proved
cause of HOL’s result and not a starvation-free guarantee.

2.4 Second-round policy evidence

SPT-HEAD-TIME is a bounded policy with a precise implementation deﬁnition. When the SPT head is
blocked, the code builds a reservation for the ﬁrst future release event at which the head has a node-
level placement witness. While that reservation is active, a backﬁll candidate is admitted only when it
is currently feasible and its ﬁnish time is no later than the promised release; a candidate that crosses
the promise is rejected by the TIME rule. The K = 200 budget counts candidate attempts only after
the blocked head has started a bypass scan. Under the shared-timer event mode, timer thresholds at
each arrival plus θ create additional dispatch events; for this policy they do not mark jobs urgent. These
statements follow the implementation recorded in the R2 supplement, which gives the exact source lines
and saved summary.
On the full A10 trace, this second-round case (full-C1, θ = 168 h, K = 200, shared timer) changes mean
waiting from 32.906454 to 32.923817 h, P99 from 1542.181042 to 1541.586592 h, and maximum from
3017.288056 to 3017.727778 h; its higher cost is not hidden by a single tail statistic. The K/timer and
HEAD-TIME results support a bounded mechanism interpretation, not starvation freedom or a general
performance theorem. The urgent policies remain preﬁx-only and are not promoted to the complete-
trace recommendation.


3    Question 3: Fixed-split cross-GPU substitution

3.1 Model and two route rules

A job has native model nj , actual model mj , duration pj , workers Wj , and per-worker requests (gj , cj ).
For the sole assumed foreign edge A10→A100-SXM4-80GB, p′j = σpj ; compatibility and σ are scenario as-
sumptions, not measured hardware facts. All workers start together on physical nodes, with no migration
or preemption, and every start passes the same CPU/GPU slot check.
The no-substitution baseline keeps each job in its native pool. The Hash-10% rule reserves 60 of 432
A100 nodes for a deterministic 10% selected A10 subset. The implemented Shape-ﬁltered rule keeps only
selected jobs whose CPU/GPU shape ﬁts the donor ratio, yielding 10,604 routed and 605 shape-rejected
A10 jobs. It is a ﬁxed physical counterfactual, not a claim that a dynamic policy has been implemented.
The formal source mapping to the corresponding C0–C4 cases and historical sensitivity cases is in the
supplement.

3.2 What the trade-off means

For SPT-HOL, Hash-10% is the more aggressive 10% split; Shape-ﬁltered rejects 605 high-CPU/GPU jobs
and changes the selected load. At the measured σ = 1 point, Hash-10% reduces A10 wait relative to No
substitution, while Shape-ﬁltered has a smaller A10 beneﬁt but lower paired positive completion-delay
mass (16,051.659 job-hours versus Hash-10% at 68,183.183 job-hours). At σ = 2, common routed-job
ordering is unchanged because all durations in one foreign queue are multiplied by the same σ; re-
simulation is still required because completion/release interactions change. Shape-ﬁltered’s A10 wait
is unchanged at 24.359 h between the two measured speeds, but A10 JCT rises 5.542 h and 90-day
completions fall by 188.
The A100 physical allocation comparison uses one full-pool denominator of 3,456 GPUs and sums native
plus foreign occupancy. Hash-10% at σ = 1 is 0.7224 (native 2,976-GPU partition 0.6276 plus foreign
480-GPU partition 0.0948). Shape-ﬁltered is 0.6899 at σ = 1 (0.7288 native plus 0.4492 foreign) and
0.7148 at σ = 2 (0.7288 plus 0.6284). Thus the foreign partition is 44.923% allocated and 55.077% idle
at σ = 1, versus 62.841% allocated and 37.159% idle at σ = 2; neither fraction is carried across scenarios.
A larger partition rate is not called a utilization gain by shrinking the native denominator.
The aggregate capacity conversion 534/8 = 66.75 is only a pooled arithmetic quantity; it is not rounded to
67 nodes and is not a node-level safe-borrow certiﬁcate. The conclusion that 60 donor nodes produce zero
native harm is supported by the formal physical schedule, while host safety still depends on node-level
placement checks.

4   Conclusion, strengths, limits, and provenance
The three questions answer different decisions. Q1 separates submitted work, immediate-start demand,
and simulated allocation. Q2 provides a capacity-valid SPT-HOL contract, a count lower bound, a zero-
wait GS1 construction, and complete-A10 tail and cost evidence. Q3 compares ﬁxed physical partitions
and measured speed assumptions while retaining native harm and duration sensitivity. These are ﬁnite
trace results: allocation is request occupancy, GPU-hours across models are not interchangeable hardware
or monetary units, and no heuristic optimality, speed guarantee, or dynamic substitution policy is claimed.
An online completion-cost routing rule remains future work.
Strengths and limitations. Strengths are exact integer-second accounting, node-level CPU/GPU gang
checks, saved-ledger replay, and separate ordinary and GPU-work objectives. Limitations are the ﬁnite
trace, request-based occupancy rather than device telemetry, unbenchmarked cross-GPU compatibility
and speed assumptions, and ﬁxed-policy rather than dynamic substitution. The reported choices are
conditional on the measured cases and do not establish a universal optimum.
