Main findings: In the supplied finite trace, A10 is the main sustained capacity-pressure pool. We
use node-level gang checks with SPT-HOL and ratio placement in GS1. HOL strongly benefits multi-
worker A10 jobs, while FILL has slightly lower mean waiting for single-worker jobs. GS1 ratio gives
zero waiting for all 9,643 jobs in this instance. Under the tested compatibility assumption and σ = 1,
dedicating 60 A100 nodes with Hash-10% routing reduces A10 mean waiting from 32.906 to 20.534
h, with no added native A100 waiting. Slower substitution retains trade-offs, and some A10 jobs finish
later. For both FCFS and SPT-HOL, Hash-10% achieves lower mean A10 JCT at σ = 1, whereas Shape-
filtered achieves lower mean A10 JCT at σ = 2. Shape-filtered also has the smaller worst-case value
across these two evaluated scenarios. These conditional choices balance mean delay, tail risk, native
harm, and donor occupancy; σ is a scenario assumption, not a measured speed.
Data and validation. We use the supplied 224,112-job trace, exact integer-second durations, six model
pools, non-preemptive gang starts, and complete drain for waiting/JCT. Allocation rates use the fixed
90-day window. Original IDs and requested fields, node-level CPU/GPU ledgers, full drain, fixed-window
counts, and independently recomputed metrics were checked against saved formal schedules.

Algorithm 1 (SPT-HOL): For each model pool, insert only arrived jobs into a shortest-processing-time
heap keyed by (pj, aj, idj). Examine the heap head. If it is infeasible on the current residual nodes, stop
dispatching this pool at this timestamp (head-of-line blocking). If feasible, scan nodes in the selected
placement order, verify every worker slot’s GPU and CPU residual, then admit all workers atomically
and schedule one completion event. For GPU-series-1 only, the main method uses a CPU/GPU-ratio
node order before the same full slot check; every other pool uses ascending-ID first fit. Advance to the
next arrival or completion and repeat.
The historical program label SJF-HOL means this same method; the paper name is uniformly SPT-HOL.
SPT-FILL uses the same key but may inspect later feasible jobs under the fixed K = 200 bypass budget.
A heap insertion or removal costs O(log n); a dispatch also scans candidate nodes and worker slots, and
ratio placement orders the candidate nodes by shape. Multiple starts at one timestamp can repeat these
scans, so an O(log n) event claim would be incomplete. Saved schedules were independently checked
by route, physical-capacity and metric validators.
The simultaneous-release mechanism has a precise necessary condition. If f -is the number of free
worker slots immediately before an event, ∆f is the number released by jobs finishing at that event,
and k is the next job width, admission requires

2.4
Second-round policy evidence
SPT-HEAD-TIME is a bounded policy with a precise implementation definition. When the SPT head is
blocked, the code builds a reservation for the first future release event at which the head has a node-
level placement witness. While that reservation is active, a backfill candidate is admitted only when it
is currently feasible and its finish time is no later than the promised release; a candidate that crosses
the promise is rejected by the TIME rule. The K = 200 budget counts candidate attempts only after
the blocked head has started a bypass scan. Under the shared-timer event mode, timer thresholds at
each arrival plus θ create additional dispatch events; for this policy they do not mark jobs urgent. These
statements follow the implementation recorded in the supplement, which gives the exact source lines
and saved summary.
On the full A10 trace, SPT-HEAD-TIME (θ = 168 h, K = 200, shared timer) changes mean wait-
ing from 32.906454 to 32.923817 h, P99 from 1542.181042 to 1541.586592 h, and maximum from
3017.288056 to 3017.727778 h; its higher cost is not hidden by a single tail statistic. The K/timer and
HEAD-TIME results support a bounded mechanism interpretation, not starvation freedom or a general
performance theorem. The urgent policies remain prefix-only and are not promoted to the complete-
trace recommendation.

3.2
What the trade-off means
For SPT-HOL, Hash-10% is the more aggressive 10% split; Shape-filtered rejects 605 high-CPU/GPU
jobs and changes the selected load. At the tested σ = 1 point, Hash-10% reduces A10 wait relative to
the No-substitution baseline, while Shape-filtered has a smaller A10 benefit but lower paired positive
completion-delay mass (16,051.659 job-hours versus Hash-10% at 68,183.183 job-hours). At σ = 2,
common routed-job ordering is unchanged because all durations in one foreign queue are multiplied
by the same σ; re-simulation is still required because completion/release interactions change. Shape-
filtered’s A10 wait is unchanged at 24.359 h between the two evaluated speeds, but A10 JCT rises 5.542
h and 90-day completions fall by 188.
The A100 allocation uses two explicit denominators. Partition-own rates are computed within each par-
tition: for Hash-10%, native/foreign are 0.728770/0.682727 at σ = 1 and 0.728770/0.669479 at σ = 2;
for Shape-filtered they are 0.728770/0.449232 and 0.728770/0.628414. The full-pool contributions are
respectively 0.627552/0.094823, 0.627552/0.092983, 0.627552/0.062393, and 0.627552/0.087280,
and each satisfies Uall = (2976Unative + 480Uforeign)/3456, giving 0.722376, 0.720535, 0.689946, and
0.714832. Thus the Shape-filtered foreign partition is 44.923% allocated and 55.077% idle at σ = 1,
versus 62.841% allocated and 37.159% idle at σ = 2; these are partition-own rates and are not full-pool
contributions. A larger partition rate is not called a utilization gain by shrinking the native denominator.
The aggregate capacity conversion 534/8 = 66.75 is only a pooled arithmetic quantity; it is not rounded
to 67 nodes and is not a node-level safe-borrow certificate. The conclusion that 60 donor nodes produce
zero native harm is supported by the formal physical schedule, while host safety still depends on node-
level placement checks.

4
Conclusion and limitations
The three questions answer different decisions. Q1 separates submitted work, immediate-start demand,
and simulated allocation. Q2 provides a capacity-valid SPT-HOL contract, a count lower bound, a zero-
wait GS1 construction, and complete-A10 tail and cost evidence. Q3 compares fixed physical partitions
and evaluated speed assumptions while retaining native harm and duration sensitivity. These are finite
trace results: allocation is request occupancy, GPU-hours across models are not interchangeable hard-
ware or monetary units, and no heuristic optimality, speed guarantee, or dynamic substitution policy is
claimed. An online completion-cost routing rule remains future work.
Strengths and limitations. Strengths are exact integer-second accounting, node-level CPU/GPU gang
checks, saved-ledger replay, and separate ordinary and GPU-work objectives. Limitations are the finite
trace, request-based occupancy rather than device telemetry, unbenchmarked cross-GPU compatibility
and speed assumptions, and fixed-policy rather than dynamic substitution. The reported choices are
conditional on the evaluated cases and do not establish a universal optimum.
