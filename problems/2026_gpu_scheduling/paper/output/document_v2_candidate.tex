\section*{Summary}
This report analyzes the supplied heterogeneous GPU trace using one capacity-valid, non-preemptive, gang-scheduling contract. The trace contains 224,112 jobs; GPU work is concentrated (Gini 0.9522), and the largest 5\% account for 87.68\% of GPU work. A10 has lifetime-work/window-capacity ratio 2.064 and counterfactual immediate-start mean demand 3,614.54 GPUs against 2,494 available; these are pressure diagnostics, not measured utilization.

For Question 2, the complete 111,284-job A10 comparison gives ordinary SPT-HOL mean waiting 32.906 h versus SPT-FILL 34.967 h, against the equal-weight job-count lower bound 22.3179305700 h. A separate protected-policy experiment is limited to a verified 65,536-job prefix. For Question 3, a declared fixed-split substitution counterfactual routes a SHA-256-selected 10\% subset to 60 dedicated A100 nodes with $\sigma=1$; it reduces A10 mean waiting on this trace while retaining native-pool harm and duration sensitivity. These are finite, contract-specific observations, not optimality or hardware-performance claims.

\section{V2 candidate integration and bounded checks}
The V2 candidate preserves the V1 trace, non-preemptive gang contract, and fixed source chain. It adds a bounded engineering check of the merged implementation, rather than a new scheduler claim. The integrated commit was tested with the current \texttt{q3.py} and shared \texttt{simulate\_pool} on the same 224,112 raw job IDs and input hashes (node file \texttt{1aba1616...9d18}; job file \texttt{ef14d696...eedb}).

Seven frozen cases were run sequentially under a 900-second and 4-GiB address-space guard: FCFS/M0 and SJF-HOL/M0 with first-fit placement; M0 with GS1 ratio placement; R0 with $\sigma=1$ and $\sigma=2$; and the two R-shape cases with $\sigma=1$ and $\sigma=2$. Each case passed route auditing, physical capacity replay, metric aggregation, and exact comparison of job timing/resource fields and placement rows against its fixed local reference. This is an implementation equivalence result for the selected cases, not proof of optimality and not a benchmark for deferred Candidate B substitution.

The comparison sources are fixed commits GS1 \texttt{ffdcc7609969...e8208}, Q2 \texttt{fcd7bafdb002...fcd0cd}, and Q3 \texttt{56ea09021d5a...115667}; complete paths and hashes are recorded in \texttt{paper\_sources.json} and the V2 runtime JSON. Q2's fourteen persisted-artifact replays independently passed the integrated validators, but their source files remain local-only; the original cross-machine persisted-replay field remains unavailable. Therefore the candidate reports technical equivalence for the fresh seven-case run and historical-account validity separately.

The V2 research branches remain candidates: K-budget/shared-timer ablation and gang placement are bounded research directions, while the substitution-v2 R-shape study is an implementation-deferred candidate. No new parameter search, scheduler, or full trace claim is introduced here.

\section{Conclusion and evidence limits}
The three questions provide complementary evidence under a declared trace and simulation contract. Q1 identifies workload concentration and two lower bounds with different weighting objectives. Q2 compares ordinary policies on the complete A10 trace and keeps protected policies at their verified prefix scope. Q3 evaluates a fixed, physical, pre-start substitution scenario while retaining native-pool harm and duration sensitivity.

Q1 uses the exact observation window $T=7,775,878$ s; scheduling waiting and JCT use complete drain; 90-day completion counts use $H=7,776,000$ s. Prefix evidence is not extended to the complete A10 trace. No unsupported performance, hardware-speed, pricing, or optimality claim is used. Large Q3 delivery archives remain local with hashes; cross-machine availability is unresolved.
