\section{Conclusion, strengths, limits, and provenance}
The three questions answer different decisions. Q1 separates submitted work, immediate-start demand, and simulated allocation. Q2 provides a capacity-valid SPT-HOL contract, a count lower bound, a zero-wait GS1 construction, and complete-A10 tail and cost evidence. Q3 compares fixed physical partitions and measured speed assumptions while retaining native harm and duration sensitivity. These are finite trace results: allocation is request occupancy, GPU-hours across models are not interchangeable hardware or monetary units, and no heuristic optimality, speed guarantee, or dynamic substitution policy is claimed. An online completion-cost routing rule remains future work.

\textbf{Strengths and limitations.} Strengths are exact integer-second accounting, node-level CPU/GPU gang checks, saved-ledger replay, and separate ordinary and GPU-work objectives. Limitations are the finite trace, request-based occupancy rather than device telemetry, unbenchmarked cross-GPU compatibility and speed assumptions, and fixed-policy rather than dynamic substitution. The reported choices are conditional on the measured cases and do not establish a universal optimum.
