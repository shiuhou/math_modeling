\section{Question 2: Capacity-Valid Scheduling}
\label{sec:q2}
\subsection{Model and common contract}
Each GPU model is simulated as a separate pool. A job starts at $s_j\ge a_j$, completes at $f_j=s_j+p_j$, and all $W_j$ workers start simultaneously. If $x_{ji}$ workers of job $j$ use node $i$, feasibility requires
\begin{equation}
 g_jx_{ji}\le G_i^{free}(t),\qquad c_jx_{ji}\le C_i^{free}(t),\qquad \sum_i x_{ji}=W_j
\end{equation}
for every active time and selected node. The DES is non-preemptive, uses exact integer-second durations, commits a gang placement atomically, and uses first-fit by numeric node ID. All results below use the same raw-data contract, \method{shared-timer} event mode, $K=200$ candidate checks per pool and timestamp, exploratory $\theta=604800$ s, and a 90-day evaluation horizon $H=7,776,000$ s. Waiting and JCT are computed after complete drain; the 90-day count is $f_j\le H$.

Ordinary \method{SPT-HOL} orders arrived jobs by $(p_j,a_j,\mathrm{id}_j)$ and stops when the head is infeasible. \method{SPT-FILL} uses the same order but may inspect later feasible jobs. For the tested fill contract, $K=200$ is a per-pool, per-timestamp bypass budget after a blocking prefix: feasible-prefix checks do not consume it, successful and failed bypass attempts do, retries may do so again, and a successful start does not reset the timestamp budget. The protected policies in the 65,536-job prefix add urgent/reservation mechanisms and are reported as a separate evidence tier; they are not relabelled as ordinary SPT-HOL.

\subsection{A job-count lower bound and accounting}
For the complete A10 set, let $C=2,494$, $G_j=W_jg_j$, and $Z(t)=\{j:a_j\le t<a_j+p_j\}$. Let $q(t)$ be the minimum number of jobs removed from $Z(t)$ so the remaining GPU width is at most $C$. Then
\begin{equation}
 L_{count}=\frac{1}{3600N}\int q(t)\,dt
\end{equation}
provides a lower bound on equal-weight mean waiting. The calculation uses exact half-open event intervals and gives 22.3179305700 h for $N=111,284$. It is a different objective from Q1's GPU-work weighted bound.

For non-preemptive schedules, the waiting variable obeys the requested bookkeeping identity $\omega_j=\min(\omega_j,p_j)+\max(\omega_j-p_j,0)$. We therefore report the within-window gap $[\sum_j\min(\omega_j,p_j)-\int q(t)dt]/(3600N)$ and the beyond-duration term $\sum_j\max(\omega_j-p_j,0)/(3600N)$. This is exact bookkeeping around a capacity relaxation, not a causal allocation of blame or a removable share.

\subsection{Complete A10 comparison}
The complete A10 evidence has 111,284 jobs and 2,494 nodes. Independent capacity replay passed for both policies. \method{SPT-HOL} has mean waiting 32.906 h, P95 11.087 h, P99 1542.181 h, maximum 3017.288 h, mean JCT 132.676 h, and 107,002 jobs completed within 90 days. \method{SPT-FILL} has mean waiting 34.967 h, P95 15.130 h, P99 1575.462 h, maximum 3015.311 h, mean JCT 134.737 h, and 106,802 90-day completions. The paired mean difference (HOL minus FILL) is -2.060576 h: 55,266 jobs improve, 50,575 worsen, and 5,443 are unchanged; 1,226 jobs that had zero wait under FILL gain positive wait under HOL. The mean advantage is therefore trace-specific and does not establish dominance or no starvation.

\subsection{Protected-prefix evidence}
The protected-policy comparison is limited to the first 65,536 A10 jobs with the same $\theta$, $K$, event mode and horizon. Its count lower bound is 24.9181888580 h. The ordinary baselines have means 30.332 h (HOL) and 31.042 h (FILL); protected means are 556.492 h (urgent HOL), 350.528 h (urgent TIME), 350.088 h (urgent FIXED), and 349.715 h (urgent FLEX). FIXED and FLEX accept 62 and 133 crossing candidates and improve mean waiting relative to TIME by 0.439 and 0.813 h, while still producing both improved and worsened jobs. This is prefix evidence and cannot be extrapolated to full A10 or the full cluster.

The cross-review revision also reports GPU-work-weighted mean waits for the same complete A10 timings: 619.352 h (FCFS), 677.690 h (SPT-HOL), and 677.496 h (SPT-FILL), using $H_j=W_jg_jp_j$ as weights. Thus the weighted objective reverses the FCFS-versus-SPT ordering seen under equal-job mean waiting. The complete-A10 evidence supports ordinary \method{SPT-HOL} as the current mean-wait main candidate, with \method{SPT-FILL} retained as a useful baseline for immediate feasible starts. The protected tier remains a bounded safety experiment rather than a selected production policy.
