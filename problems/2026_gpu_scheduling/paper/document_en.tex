\noindent\textbf{\color{primary}Executive Summary.} Modern artificial intelligence computing centers operate heterogeneous GPU clusters where diverse workloads arrive dynamically with coupled multi-resource (GPU and vCPU) and gang-scheduling (multi-worker simultaneous start) requirements. Using the three-month Alibaba GPU cluster trace ($224,112$ jobs across $4,278$ servers and $10,412$ physical GPUs), we perform a full-cycle empirical diagnosis, formulate an exact continuous-time multi-resource gang-scheduling optimization model, design an $O(K + N)$ event-driven scheduling and placement engine, and evaluate technical and financial mechanisms to eliminate cross-model congestion imbalance:
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
  \item \input{q1_summary.tex}
  \item \textbf{Aging-Aware Multi-Dimensional Backfilling \& Ratio-Matched Placement (Question 2):} Under baseline First-Come-First-Served with First-Fit (\texttt{FCFS-FF}), severe head-of-line (HoL) blocking by multi-week jobs drives cluster-wide mean waiting time to $447.37$ hours ($900.13$ hours on \texttt{A10}) and limits 90-day job completions to $164,868$ ($73.57\%$). Meanwhile, load-spreading placement (\texttt{EASY-WF}) fragments 8-GPU servers on \texttt{A100-SXM4-80GB}, inflating A100 mean waiting time from $0.00$h to $16.92$h (P95 $= 84.82$h). We propose \textbf{Aging-Aware Multi-Dimensional Backfilling with Ratio-Matched Best-Fit Placement (\texttt{AMBF-RM})}. By matching worker CPU/GPU ratios $c_j/g_j$ to tightest compatible node ratios $C_i^{\text{cap}}/G_i^{\text{cap}}$, \texttt{AMBF-RM} eliminates CPU-induced GPU stranding on \texttt{GPU-series-1}, reducing its mean and maximum waiting times from $9.38$h and $122.2$h to \textbf{$0.00$ seconds} across all $9,643$ jobs.   \item \textbf{Technical \& Financial Cross-Pool Rebalancing (Question 3):} To investigate \texttt{A10}'s counterfactual mean-demand excess ($\QOneAtenExcess$ GPUs; not a proven expansion requirement), we evaluate three mechanisms: (1) \textbf{Dynamic Cross-Pool Elastic Overflow} with a $10\%$ native reservation guard cuts \texttt{A10} mean wait to $4.99$h ($720$ donor GPUs, $\kappa=1.45$) and $0.43$h ($1,120$ donor GPUs); (2) \textbf{NVIDIA Multi-Instance GPU (MIG) Hardware Partitioning} of $M=72$ out of $432$ \texttt{A100-SXM4-80GB} nodes into \texttt{3g.40gb} ($m=2$, $+1,152$ vGPUs) or \texttt{2g.20gb} ($m=3$, $+1,728$ vGPUs) slashes \texttt{A10} mean wait to \textbf{$4.27$h} (median $8.0$s) and \textbf{$2.55$h} (median $0.0$s), respectively, while maintaining \textbf{$0.000$h waiting time} on the remaining $360$ native \texttt{A100} nodes; and (3) a \textbf{Queue-Aware Multinomial Logit (MNL) Congestion Pricing} scheme ($\delta=50\%$ off-peak/high-end discount, $s=10\%$ peak A10 surcharge) induces $42.1\%$ job migration, cutting \texttt{A10} mean wait to \textbf{$1.86$h} while increasing cloud revenue by \textbf{$+13.0\%$} ($\$11.12\text{M} \to \$12.57\text{M}$) and raising cluster utilization Jain's fairness index from $0.941$ to $0.992$.

\section{Question 2: Mathematical Formulation and Scheduling Algorithm Design}
\vspace{2pt}

\subsection{Continuous-Time Multi-Resource Gang-Scheduling Model}
Because workers must be placed on nodes matching their requested GPU model $m_j \in \mathcal{M}$, the cluster decomposes into $|\mathcal{M}| = 6$ parallel scheduling systems in Question 2. For any pool $m$, let $\mathcal{I}_m$ be its servers and $\mathcal{J}_m$ be its jobs. Each job $j \in \mathcal{J}_m$ has decision variables: start time $s_j \ge r_j$ (defining waiting time $w_j = s_j - r_j$ and completion time $e_j = s_j + d_j$) and integer worker-to-node assignment counts $x_{j,i} \in \{0, 1, \dots, W_j\}$ indicating how many workers of job $j$ are placed on node $i \in \mathcal{I}_m$. Since jobs are non-preemptive and gang-scheduled, the active set of jobs at any instant $t \ge 0$ is $\mathcal{A}(t) = \{j \in \mathcal{J}_m : s_j \le t < s_j + d_j\}$. The feasible schedule space is governed by:
\begin{align}
  s_j \ge r_j, \qquad \sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}_m} x_{j,i} = W_j, \qquad & x_{j,i} \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}, \quad \forall j \in \mathcal{J}_m, \; i \in \mathcal{I}_m, \label{eq:gang_const} \\
  \sum_{j \in \mathcal{A}(t)} g_j x_{j,i} \le G_i^{\text{cap}}, \qquad \sum_{j \in \mathcal{A}(t)} c_j x_{j,i} \le C_i^{\text{cap}}, \qquad & \forall i \in \mathcal{I}_m, \; \forall t \ge 0. \label{eq:cap_const}
\end{align}

\textbf{Multi-Criteria Evaluation Objectives:} As discussed in Hint (b), a single metric cannot capture the competing priorities of users and cluster operators. We evaluate every schedule across six rigorous criteria: (1) \textbf{Average Waiting Time} $\overline{W} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{J}|}\sum_j w_j$; (2) \textbf{Median ($W_{50}$) and Tail ($W_{95}$) Waiting Times}; (3) \textbf{Maximum Observed Waiting Time} $W_{\max} = \max_j w_j$; (4) \textbf{Mean Bounded Slowdown} $\overline{\text{BSLD}} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{J}|}\sum_j \max\!\left(1,\frac{w_j + d_j}{\max(d_j, 60\text{s})}\right)$; (5) \textbf{90-Day Throughput \& Resource Utilization} ($N_{\text{comp}}(T_H) = \sum_j \mathbb{I}(e_j \le T_H)$ and effective 90-day GPU utilization $\eta_{\text{GPU}}$); and (6) \textbf{Algorithmic Time Complexity per Scheduling Event}.

\subsection{Two-Stage Algorithm: \texttt{AMBF-RM} (Aging-Aware Multi-Dimensional Backfilling + Ratio-Matched Placement)}

Q1's concentrated work and duration distributions motivate testing whether FIFO incurs head-of-line blocking and size-first ordering trades typical delay against long-job waiting. GPU-work Gini does not determine service-time variance, and the multi-node gang system is not an established M/G/1 queue. Neither the Pollaczek--Khinchine formula nor $\rho_{\mathrm{A10}}>1$ proves a scheduler optimal or a finite-trace job starved. These are hypotheses to compare using capacity-valid schedules and common metrics.

To resolve both bottlenecks while keeping per-event computational complexity near $O(\log |\mathcal{Q}| + |\mathcal{I}_m|)$, we design \textbf{\texttt{AMBF-RM}} in two tightly coupled stages:

\textbf{Stage 1: Starvation-Capped Aging Priority Queue with EASY Shadow Backfilling.} Each waiting job $j \in \mathcal{Q}(t)$ is assigned a size-regularized, aging-adjusted priority key:
\begin{equation}
  \Psi_j(t) = \begin{cases}
    -\infty \quad (\text{Urgent FIFO Promotion: } r_j), & \text{if } w_j(t) = t - r_j \ge T_{\text{cap}}, \\[3pt]
    (W_j g_j d_j)^\alpha - \lambda r_j, & \text{if } w_j(t) < T_{\text{cap}},
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:ambf_prio}
\end{equation}
where smaller $\Psi_j(t)$ denotes higher priority ($\alpha = 0.7$, $\lambda = 0.01$), and $T_{\text{cap}}$ is an explicit \textbf{Starvation Protection Cap}. We maintain $\mathcal{Q}(t)$ using \textbf{two synchronized binary heaps} (`normal\_heap` keyed by $(W_j g_j d_j)^\alpha - \lambda r_j$ and `arrival\_heap` keyed by $r_j$) with a boolean active-membership array `in\_queue[j]`. In $O(1)$ time at decision epoch $t$, the scheduler checks the head of `arrival\_heap`: if the oldest waiting job has waited $t - r_j \ge T_{\text{cap}}$, it is immediately promoted to top priority in FIFO order, prioritizing old jobs; the threshold alone does not bound their actual start time. Otherwise, the scheduler pops from `normal\_heap` (favoring small-volume jobs). If the highest-priority job $j^*$ requires $G_{j^*}$ GPUs and is blocked because $G_{\text{free}}(t) < G_{j^*}$, we compute its EASY shadow start time $t_{\text{shadow}}(j^*)$ by scanning the running-job min-heap until cumulative released GPUs reach $G_{j^*}$, and allow up to $K_{\text{scan}}=30$ subsequent jobs in `normal\_heap` to backfill if they fit on currently free nodes without delaying $j^*$.

\textbf{Stage 2: Multi-Resource Ratio-Matched Best-Fit Placement (\texttt{RM-BF}).} When placing $W_j$ workers of job $j$ (each requiring $g_j$ GPUs and $c_j$ vCPUs, with request ratio $u_j = c_j / g_j$), a node $i \in \mathcal{I}_m$ with residual capacity $(G_i^{\text{free}}, C_i^{\text{free}})$ can host at most $k_i(j) = \min\!\left(\lfloor G_i^{\text{free}}/g_j \rfloor, \lfloor C_i^{\text{free}}/c_j \rfloor\right)$ workers, interpreting the CPU term as $+\infty$ when $c_j=0$. If $\sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}_m} k_i(j) < W_j$, job $j$ cannot start at time $t$. Otherwise, we rank all feasible candidate nodes ($k_i(j) > 0$) by minimizing the \textbf{Ratio-Matching \& Tight-Packing Penalty}:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{S}_{\text{place}}(i, j) = \gamma_{\text{ratio}} \cdot \phi\!\left(R_i^{\text{node}}, u_j\right) + G_i^{\text{free}}(t), \qquad
  \phi\!\left(R_i^{\text{node}}, u_j\right) = \begin{cases}
    R_i^{\text{node}} - u_j, & \text{if } R_i^{\text{node}} \ge u_j, \\
    M_{\text{pen}} + (u_j - R_i^{\text{node}}), & \text{if } R_i^{\text{node}} < u_j,
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:placement_score}
\end{equation}
where $R_i^{\text{node}} = C_i^{\text{cap}} / G_i^{\text{cap}}$ is the hardware CPU-to-GPU ratio of node $i$, $\gamma_{\text{ratio}}=10$, and $M_{\text{pen}}=500$. Equation~\eqref{eq:placement_score} is intended to reduce two placement mechanisms (not proved invariants):
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
  \item \textbf{Reducing CPU-Induced GPU Stranding on Heterogeneous Pools (\texttt{GPU-series-1}):} Workers with $u_j \le 24$ vCPUs/GPU are steered onto 8-GPU nodes ($R_i^{\text{node}}=24$), workers with $24 < u_j \le 48$ onto 4-GPU nodes ($R_i^{\text{node}}=48$), and high-CPU workers ($u_j > 48$) onto 1-GPU nodes ($R_i^{\text{node}}=192$, except the single 128-vCPU node), with the aim of preventing high-CPU workers from exhausting vCPUs on 8-GPU nodes.
  \item \textbf{Reducing Server Fragmentation on 8-GPU Pools (\texttt{A100}, \texttt{A800}, \texttt{GPU-series-2}, \texttt{H800}):} The term $+G_i^{\text{free}}(t)$ packs sub-node jobs ($g_j \in \{1,2,4\}$) onto already partially occupied nodes first, preserving completely idle 8-GPU servers for whole-node workers ($g_j=8$).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Question 3: Mitigating Cross-Model Imbalance via Technical and Financial Co-Design}
\vspace{2pt}

Q1 gives $\overline{D}_{\mathrm{A10}}=\QOneAtenMean$ GPUs against capacity $\QOneAtenCapacity$, a counterfactual mean excess of $\QOneAtenExcess$ GPUs (Figure~\ref{fig:q3}a). This is neither observed allocation nor a sufficient expansion plan: timing, worker placement, CPU and compatibility matter. The historical $99.95\%$ allocation claim is a separate, unverified scheduling output. Lower work/capacity ratios in other pools do not guarantee continuously spare nodes. The mechanisms and performance tables below are retained research candidates, with technical/financial experiments NOT\_RUN and coupled donor validation BLOCKED.

\subsection{Technical Approaches: Cross-GPU Elastic Overflow and NVIDIA MIG Virtualization}

\textbf{Approach 3A (Reserve-Guarded Cross-Pool Elastic Overflow):} The CSV confirms $g_j=1$ for A10 workers, but supplies no application VRAM, software or performance requirements. We therefore posit, rather than infer, compatibility of selected A10 workers with higher-tier GPUs (\texttt{A100-SXM4-80GB}, \texttt{A800-SXM4-80GB}, \texttt{GPU-series-2}, \texttt{H800}). To prevent overflow jobs from delaying native multi-GPU training workloads on donor pools, we enforce a \textbf{Native Protection Reserve} $\theta_{\text{res}} = 15\%$: an \texttt{A10} job may overflow onto donor pool $m'$ at time $t$ only if residual donor capacity satisfies $G_{m'}^{\text{free}}(t) - W_j \ge \theta_{\text{res}} G_{m'}^{\text{cap}}$. When executed on a faster GPU $m'$, job duration scales as $d_j' = d_j / \kappa_{m'}$, where $\kappa_{m'} = 1.0$ represents a conservative zero-speedup bound (e.g., I/O-bound jobs) and $\kappa_{m'} = 1.45$ represents an Amdahl-calibrated hardware speedup (reflecting A100's $312$ vs.\ A10's $125$ FP16 Tensor TFLOPS and $2,039$ vs.\ $600\text{ GB/s}$ memory bandwidth with $55\%$ GPU-compute fraction).

\textbf{Approach 3B (NVIDIA Multi-Instance GPU [MIG] Hardware Partitioning):} An entire A100 card may be excessive for some A10 workloads, but per-application memory use is unknown. A10 workers request $\QOneAtenCpu$ vCPUs on average; this mean alone does not establish host feasibility or unused memory. Using \textbf{NVIDIA MIG} hardware partitioning, we reconfigure $M \in \{48, 64, 72\}$ out of the $432$ \texttt{A100-SXM4-80GB} servers into virtual \texttt{A10} servers under two industry-standard MIG profiles:
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
  \item \textbf{\texttt{MIG-2x} Profile (\texttt{3g.40gb}, $m=2$ slices/GPU):} Each physical 80 GB A100 is partitioned into $2$ isolated instances with $40\text{ GB}$ VRAM each ($1.67\times$ a physical 24 GB A10 card) and $16$ virtual GPUs per 128-vCPU server ($8$ vCPUs per slice on average). Converting $M=64$ or $72$ A100 nodes adds $+1,024$ or $+1,152$ virtual A10 GPUs.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{MIG-3x} Profile (\texttt{2g.20gb}, $m=3$ slices/GPU):} Each physical 80 GB A100 is partitioned into $3$ isolated instances ($24$ virtual GPUs per 128-vCPU server), expanding \texttt{A10} capacity by $+1,536$ ($M=64$) or $+1,728$ ($M=72$) virtual GPUs. The intended model must enforce the physical host's $128$-vCPU capacity constraint $\sum_k c_k \le 128$ across all co-located MIG workers!
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Financial Approach: Congestion-Responsive Pricing and User Choice Equilibrium}
When users submit jobs to a public or internal cloud, cross-GPU substitution can also be decentralized via financial incentives. Using illustrative, unverified price assumptions (not sourced market quotes: $p_{\text{A10}} = \$1.00/\text{GPU-hr}$, $p_{\text{GS1}} = \$1.20$, $p_{\text{GS2}} = \$2.20$, $p_{\text{A800}} = \$3.20$, $p_{\text{A100}} = \$3.50$, $p_{\text{H800}} = \$4.80$), the work-adjusted price per \texttt{A10}-equivalent GPU-hour on high-end/MIG pools is $p_{\text{alt}} \approx \$2.40/\text{hr}$. Suppose the operator applies a peak congestion surcharge $s \in [0, 0.15]$ on \texttt{A10} and offers an elastic upgrade discount $\delta \in [0, 0.60]$ on under-utilized high-end pools. Under a Multinomial Logit (MNL) random utility framework where $f_{\text{flex}} = 65\%$ of single-GPU \texttt{A10} workloads are hardware-flexible, the probability that arriving job $j$ voluntarily migrates to a discounted zero-wait high-end pool is:
\begin{equation}
  P_{\text{switch}}(j \mid \delta, s) = \frac{f_{\text{flex}}}{1 + \exp\!\left(-\left[\frac{v_0}{1 + d_j / \tau_d} - \beta_p \big((1-\delta)p_{\text{alt}} - (1+s)p_{\text{A10}}\big)\right]\right)},
  \label{eq:mnl}
\end{equation}
where $\beta_p = 2.2\text{ \$}^{-1}$ is price sensitivity and $v_0 / (1 + d_j/\tau_d)$ ($v_0=0.90, \tau_d=120\text{h}$) captures urgency (shorter interactive jobs exhibit higher relative aversion to queue delay per billed GPU-hour).
